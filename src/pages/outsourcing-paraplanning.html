What to ask before you outsource paraplanning.

Outsourcing moves the preparation work outside your practice. It does not move the responsibility.

The short answer

  • The adviser and the licensee stay accountable for the advice, whoever prepares the document.
  • Judge a provider on how it answers the eight questions below, in writing, rather than on claims about speed.
  • The most useful answers are specific: who reviews, what is sent where, what comes back, and what is left for you.

1. What exactly will they prepare?

Paraplanning covers a range of work: Statements of Advice (SOAs), Records of Advice (ROAs), client summaries, file notes, research and strategy documentation, and document review. Ask which of these are in scope, which templates the provider will work to, and what is excluded. A good answer confirms scope and deliverables before work starts and works to your templates and standards.

2. Who checks the work before it comes back?

Ask whether a person reviews every document, and whether technology, including AI, is used in preparation. If it is, ask what the technology does and what the human reviewer checks. A good answer names a human review step for every document, and explains how information in the draft is checked against the client file and your instructions.

3. What stays with the adviser?

The recommendations and the sign-off. A provider that prepares your documents should not be giving personal advice to your clients. A good answer states plainly that the adviser reviews the recommendations and retains responsibility for final approval.

4. Where does client information go?

Ask where files are stored, who can access them, whether any provider outside Australia receives them, and whether AI services retain them. Ask where the people doing the work are based. Neither answer is automatically right. What matters is that you know, that your licensee accepts it, and that it is consistent with the Privacy Act. If information goes overseas, the cross-border disclosure rules (APP 8) may apply to you too, so check with your compliance adviser. A good answer is specific and matches the provider’s published privacy policy.

5. How is it priced, and what is included?

Providers can price per document, per hour or on a retainer. The headline rate matters less than what it includes: revisions, urgent turnaround, ROAs, file notes and research. A good answer lists inclusions and exclusions so two quotes can be compared like for like.

6. What happens when information is missing?

Client files rarely arrive complete. Ask how gaps and conflicting figures are handled. A good answer is that they are raised as queries and listed in a handover note, not filled by assumption.

7. What happens when your volume changes?

Advice requests and review deadlines do not arrive evenly. Ask how capacity is agreed, what turnaround you can expect for different documents, and what happens at peak times. A good answer sets expectations in the service agreement, rather than promising a single number for every file.

8. Does it fit your licensee’s requirements?

Your licensee may have templates, approved product lists, file standards and rules on outsourcing and AI use. ASIC has reminded licensees that they remain accountable for functions they outsource. A good answer shows how the provider works within your licensee’s requirements rather than around them. compliance lawyer to confirm the outsourcing wording before launch
